# 365 Copilot Prompting Starter Guide (FinBridge)

Date: 2026-08-12  
Audience: Non-technical office staff  
Style: Practical, copy-ready examples

## Why this guide
Copilot works best when your request is clear and specific. This guide gives you ready-to-use prompts for day-to-day work in Outlook, Word, and Excel.

---

## The easy formula for a good prompt
Use this 4-part formula every time:

**Ask + Context + Output + Deadline**

- Ask: What you want Copilot to do
- Context: Which emails/files/period/team this relates to
- Output: The format you want (bullet list, draft email, table, etc.)
- Deadline: Time window or urgency

Quick template you can reuse:

"Please [ASK]. Use [CONTEXT]. Return as [OUTPUT]. Focus on [DEADLINE/PRIORITY]."

Example:

"Please summarize this email thread. Use only messages from the last 7 days. Return as 5 bullet points plus 3 action items. Focus on what must be done before Friday 3 PM."

## Outlook prompt examples (copy and use)

### Example 1: Inbox catch-up after leave
"Review my unread emails from the last 3 working days and group them into: urgent today, this week, and FYI. For urgent today, draft short reply options I can send in under 2 lines."

### Example 2: Prepare for a manager check-in
"Summarize my emails with the Finance Ops team from this week. Keep it simple: what was decided, what still needs an answer, and what is blocked. Finish with a short speaking note I can read in 1 minute before my 2 PM meeting."

### Example 3: Action-focused meeting follow-up
"From this email thread, draft a follow-up email I can send to the team. Confirm decisions, say who is doing each action, and include due dates. Keep it polite and clear."

---

## Word prompt examples (copy and use)

### Example 1: Turn notes into a polished update
"Using the notes in this document, draft a one-page Finance status update for senior managers. Include sections: highlights, risks, decisions needed, and next steps. Keep language plain and concise."

### Example 2: Rewrite for clarity
"Rewrite this draft so it is easier to understand for non-finance colleagues. Keep all key facts the same, shorten long sentences, and replace jargon with simple words."

### Example 3: Executive summary creation
"Create an executive summary from this document in 8 bullet points. Put the most important business impact in the first 2 bullets. End with 3 recommended actions for this month."

---

## Excel prompt examples (copy and use)

### Example 1: Monthly variance summary
"Look at this table and show the biggest spend changes by category from last month to this month. List the top 5 increases and top 5 decreases, with amount and percentage, in a simple table."

### Example 2: Outlier check
"Review this worksheet and highlight numbers that look unusually high or low for each category. Suggest likely reasons and tell me the first 3 checks I should do."

### Example 3: Management-ready narrative
"Based on this sheet, write a short narrative for a management update: what improved, what worsened, and where we need action. Keep it to 6 bullet points and include exact figures."

## Quick prompt upgrades when results are too generic
If Copilot gives a vague answer, add one or more of these:

- Time scope: "Use only data from April to June 2026"
- Audience: "Write for senior leadership" or "write for new starters"
- Length: "Maximum 120 words" or "exactly 5 bullet points"
- Priority: "Focus on risks first"
- Source boundary: "Use only this document" or "Use only this email thread"

## Data safety reminder (Day 1 habit)
From Day 1 of this program: keep client and company data safe by default.

- Good news: you can use Microsoft 365 Copilot confidently inside FinBridge's approved environment.
- Keep sensitive data in approved FinBridge tools only. Do not paste confidential client data, payroll data, board materials, or M&A details into external AI tools.
- If you are unsure whether a tool is approved, pause and ask DWP/IT Security. It is always okay to check first.

Simple rule: **If the data is sensitive, keep it inside approved FinBridge systems only.**

## One-minute checklist before you prompt
- Am I in the correct M365 app (Outlook, Word, Excel)?
- Did I state exactly what output I want?
- Did I give enough context (time period, team, file, thread)?
- Did I avoid sharing sensitive data into unapproved external tools?

If all four are yes, your prompt is ready.
